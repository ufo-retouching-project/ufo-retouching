## Agreed editing standard

- Top to bottom without a replacement whip: `CC`, `RT`, `White Background`, `Original`. With a replacement whip: `CC`, `RT`, `Black Whip` or `White Whip`, `White Background`, `Original`.
- `Original` preserves the untouched source image, hidden and locked. Never save changes over a source or reference PSD.
- `RT` is a cleaned duplicate with an editable whole-subject mask. Use localized cleanup for clear scratches, dust, and specks, and for the label removals described below. Preserve texture, seams, the CCT label, LED colors, and real product details. Leave uncertain marks and report their locations.
- `CC` contains named component groups, each with its own editable mask. Group by parts that are adjusted independently, not every physical piece. For UFO/high-bay fixtures, use one `Fixture` group and mask for the connected housing, rim, and controls; these areas are normally adjusted together. Keep parts that may need separate treatment—such as reflector, LEDs, lens, sensor, CCT label, and screws—in their own groups when present. Do not create separate `Housing`, `Rim`, or `Controls` masks in the standard UFO workflow. Use only parts actually present; infer visually before asking.
- No appearance-changing adjustment is part of this first phase.
- Component masks must follow the actual visible contours at full resolution. A circle, ellipse, or broad polygon can be a starting selection, but must be refined to the photographed part before acceptance. Inspect perspective, irregular edges, recesses, openings, and adjoining parts; avoid including surrounding housing in a screw mask. Smooth-looking geometry alone is not evidence of an accurate mask.
- `Hardware` contains screws only by default, grouped together under one independently editable mask. Silver/metallic appearance is a useful identification cue, but does not make the mounting ring, housing, bracket, or label hardware. Include other hardware only when the user identifies it as such.
- Remove silver labels/stickers from ordinary product shots with localized cloning/healing that follows the underlying surface texture and lighting. Preserve these labels in a dedicated label shot; identify that shot from the image and ask only if ambiguous. Keep and precisely mask the front label listing color temperatures (the `CCT label`). A label removed from the retouched view should not remain in a component mask.
- `White Background` is a separate white layer, visible by default. Hiding it must reveal an isolated subject on transparency.
- For UFOs and high bays, always remove all photographed cables and replace them with the correct whip, whether or not there are red X marks. This includes the second loose cable in UFO 5. A photographer's red X confirms removal; do not retain the annotation as an uncertain mark. Exclude removed cables and annotations from the subject and component masks, and reconstruct the fixture only where necessary at the attachment. Preserve the untouched cable pixels in `Original`. Other fixture categories need their own applicable cable rules.
- Choose the replacement whip by fixture finish: `Black_Whip copy.psd` for a black fixture and `White_Whip copy.psd` for a white fixture. Infer the finish visually; ask if neither match is clear. Preserve these reusable asset PSDs.
- Position the whip at the fixture's upper right. Match the finished examples' relative scale, orientation, and attachment: the connecting tail meets or disappears behind the upper-right fixture housing, with the coil above/right of it and the exposed wires visible. Use relative placement for each image, not fixed UFO 4 coordinates. `UFO 2 Finished.psd` demonstrates the black whip; `UFO 3 Finished.psd` demonstrates the white whip. Their composite previews have been visually inspected.

## Component selection method

- Confirmed by the user on October 5, 2026: start component masks with Photoshop selections rather than tracing every component from scratch. Use Object Selection or Quick Selection for the combined `Fixture` region and other visually coherent parts; use color-based selections, such as Color Range or Magic Wand, where a component has a distinct color. Choose the method from the photographed part's appearance and inspect the result rather than assuming it worked.
- Constrain each selection to the intended component or local region. The `Fixture` mask includes the connected housing, rim, and controls, but excludes separately editable lens, LED, sensor, label, and hardware regions where those parts are present. A color match alone does not establish component identity; exclude similarly colored neighboring parts. Combine the individually selected screws into the single `Hardware` mask.
- Refine the initial selection with Select and Mask as appropriate, then add or subtract only missed or incorrectly included areas. Inspect full-resolution contours, openings, occlusions, and adjoining parts. Preserve the existing precision standard; broad feathering or smoothing must not conceal inaccurate boundaries.
- Use brush, lasso, pen/path, or scripted contour corrections only where selection and refinement leave errors. Do not default to coordinate tracing of every component. If a component requires substantial tracing, report the selection method attempted and the observed limitation.
- Save the resulting selections as independently editable component group masks and export the full-canvas mask assets required by the apply job. For UFO/high-bay jobs, export one `Fixture` mask rather than separate housing, rim, and controls masks. Whole-fixture Select Subject does not replace the `Fixture` mask plus the other independently editable component masks.
- The current queue importer consumes prepared component masks; these instructions do not mean it already performs Object Selection, Quick Selection, or Select and Mask for components. Use native Photoshop controls or extend and verify the plugin as needed to execute this method. If a native operation cannot be accessed reliably, report that limitation and use localized corrections where feasible; do not silently revert to tracing all components.
- Reuse the method, tools, and approved editing rules across photographs, not photo-specific selection coordinates. Track selection/refinement time separately from cleanup, Photoshop execution, and verification on fresh-image trials so speed improvements are measured rather than assumed. Avoid repeating near-identical selection prompts: after two results cross the same boundary, change selection method or use a localized correction and record the limitation.
- Efficiency target: keep one source inspection, one planned selection pass, and one focused full-resolution correction/review pass where feasible. Batch compatible Photoshop operations and mask exports. Do not regenerate already accepted masks or rerun setup for a local correction. If a mask remains uncertain after the focused correction pass, preserve the best editable candidate, flag the exact area, and report elapsed time by stage instead of silently spending another long loop. Accuracy requirements still apply; this is a workflow-efficiency rule, not permission to accept visibly wrong contours.
- `scripts/prepare_ufo4_refinement.py` is an existing photo-specific trial implementation, not the default component selection method going forward.
